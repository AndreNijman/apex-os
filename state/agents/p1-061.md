# p1-061 — Extend APEX capabilities to normal application permissions

Task: **P1-061** (`todo`), depends on P1-001 (done). Repo: apex-os with an
apex-shell half. Branch `task/p1-061-app-permissions`, from
`origin/roadmap/v2.2`. Worktree `/var/tmp/apex-work/wt-p1-061`.

## Criteria, quoted from roadmap.yaml

1. A design unifies or brokers filesystem, camera, microphone, screen capture,
   USB/device, network, sensitive-directory and secret access **where
   Linux/portal primitives permit**.
2. Flatpak and native apps can be represented **without pretending they have
   identical enforcement mechanisms**.
3. Settings → Privacy & Permissions shows effective capabilities and origin.
4. Revocation behaviour is explicit and tested.

Criteria 1 and 2 both contain their own honesty clause, and they are the whole
difficulty of this item. A Flatpak's camera access is enforced by the portal and
the sandbox; a native binary's is enforced by nothing at all. A page that shows
both as "allowed" with the same tick is a lie about the machine, and it is the
lie this item exists to prevent.

## What already exists — read before designing

- `apex-secretd` is the brokered-credential precedent: capability sets per
  caller, and a broker that hands out no token. Section 13's operation
  vocabulary is the shape a capability name takes in this system.
- `privilege::decide` and `origin::classify` in `apex-agentd` already answer
  "who is asking, and from where" with seven request origins. App permissions
  are a second axis on the same question, not a new mechanism.
- `apex-agent-core/src/mcpconf.rs` gained a four-value `Wrap` type in P1-026
  precisely because `Option<bool>` could not say "in the definition" versus "at
  launch". Effective-capability reporting needs the same care: "allowed",
  "allowed but unenforceable" and "not asked" are three answers, not two.
- xdg-desktop-portal is what a Flatpak goes through. Find out which portal
  backend this image ships and what it can actually revoke before promising
  revocation.
